Every marketing team using AI has the same conversation eventually: "Why doesn't this sound like us?"

The copy is fine. Grammatically correct. Hits the brief. But something's off. It could be any brand's work. So you tweak the prompt, add more instructions, maybe paste in a paragraph from your tone of voice guidelines. It gets a little better. Then the next person on your team writes a different prompt and you're back to square one.

## The prompting trap

It's easy to assume better prompts will fix this. After all, that's how AI works — you tell it what you want and it delivers. So if the output isn't right, you must not be asking correctly.

But there's a limit to what prompting can do. Every prompt starts from zero. Your AI doesn't remember that you prefer sentence case headlines, or that "innovative" is on your list of banned words, or that your brand voice is warm but never casual. You have to tell it again, every time, and hope everyone on your team tells it the same way.

This isn't a prompting problem. It's a context problem.

## What context actually means

Brand context isn't just your guidelines document converted to a system prompt. It's the full picture of what makes your brand yours:

- **Positioning**: Where you sit in the market and why that matters
- **Voice**: How you sound, including the things you'd never say
- **Visual identity**: Not just colours and fonts, but *why* those choices
- **Proof**: The evidence that backs up your claims
- **Design principles**: What makes something feel right or wrong

Most AI tools can read a PDF. Few can form an opinion. Fewer still can tell you *why* something works or doesn't — the kind of feedback a good creative director would give.

## The creative director test

Here's a useful benchmark: would a creative director catch it?

A creative director notices when a headline breaks your capitalisation rules. They spot when copy uses a word that's off-brand, even if it's technically correct. They ask whether a design choice was deliberate before calling it a mistake.

That's the standard your AI should meet. Not perfect compliance with a checklist, but genuine understanding of what your brand is trying to do — enough to make judgement calls.
