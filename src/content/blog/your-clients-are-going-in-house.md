If you run an agency, you've felt this one coming. Clients bringing production in-house. Buying the tools, hiring a junior who's quick with them, doing the work they used to send you.

The instinct is to treat it as a loss. Resist it, out-produce it, quietly hope it doesn't catch on.

Wrong instinct. The clients going in-house are doing exactly what you'd do in their position, and fighting it is a fast way to become the thing they're replacing. The agencies that come out ahead are the ones helping them do it, because that's where the actual value moved to.

## What they get, and what they don't

Here's what in-house AI gives a client. Speed. Volume. The ability to turn something around on a Tuesday afternoon without a purchase order. That's real, and no agency retainer competes with it on those terms.

Here's what it doesn't give them. The judgement to know which of the ten things it produced is the right one. A view on whether an idea is any good, as opposed to merely finished. Someone who can look at a quarter's worth of output and say this is drifting, this is working, do more of that. The tool makes the work. It has no opinion about whether the work should have been made.

That gap doesn't shrink as the tools improve. It grows. The more a team can produce, the more they need someone who can tell them what's worth producing. You've been that person for years. The job didn't disappear. It got more important and moved up the chain.

## From pairs of hands to the thing in the loop

So the question isn't how to stop clients going in-house. It's what you sell them once they have.

Not production. They've got that. What they'll pay for is direction. The thinking that shapes what they make before they make it, and the judgement that checks it after. You stop being the pair of hands and become the thing in the loop: the creative mind their fast, capable engine is missing.

That's a better business than the one you had. Higher up the value chain, harder to replace, and it scales in a way that doing the work by hand never did. You're no longer selling hours. You're selling the one thing their tools can't generate.

The clients leaving to go in-house aren't walking away from you. They're walking towards needing exactly what you're actually good at. Meet them there.
